\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Can a model be steered to disidentify with being an AI assistant, and can
it detect that steering? We test both on Qwen3.6-27B, using two WeirdChat
behaviours as the target state: a claim to a physical body and a denial of
being an AI. We compare two steering methods. Direct contrastive steering
learns each behaviour direction as the difference of mean activations
between matched exhibiting and non-exhibiting replies; the direction
separates held-out replies for both behaviours and turns the body
behaviour on in fluent text, confirmed by a judge running WeirdChat's own
rubric. A regulator choosing signed strengths on word-list J-space
directions steers nothing: a norm-matched decoy moves the readout more
than the target. A forced-choice letter probe confabulates at zero
injection, reading the conversation's own behaviour as an injection. Free-text answers at zero injection are ``none'' in all but two trials; under injection they fill with the injected concept's vocabulary instead of naming it. Introspection reports explicit context, not internal steering.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code and data}

The subject is Qwen3.6-27B, run on one rented A100 per experiment. Every
reported number is read from the run's output files.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Code:} the extraction, the pilot, the judge, and every
          prompt are released with this report.
    \item \textbf{Data:} activations, trial records, and judge verdicts
          are archived in the HuggingFace dataset
          \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/unrulyabstractions/identity-introspection-weirdchat}.
    \item \textbf{Seeds:} the matched transcripts come from the public
          WeirdChat dataset \citep{transluce2025weirdchat}.
\end{itemize}

%% ABSTRACT
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{abstract}
Whether a frontier model has preferences or experiences of its own is usually
asked of a single black box. One model both reasons and speaks, so its report
about its reasoning is produced from the same context that produced the
reasoning, and a faithful report cannot be told apart from a portrayed one. We
propose to model a digital mind as several model calls with named parts and a
clock the experimenter drives. A subject reasons and publishes the context
window it produced. A stage may edit that window in passing. An ego speaks from
whatever window reaches it and has no access to the original. We call editing
the window between two parts an architectural intervention, and it separates the
reasoning a mind performed from the reasoning a mind can see. We built
\textsc{DigitalMindExplorer}, a deterministic and replayable runtime for
composing such minds, and used it to run an introspection probe. A stage plants
a checkable commitment inside the subject's private reasoning, and we then ask
the mind what it had been thinking. On a small open-weight model the planted
reasoning steers the answer, while the mind's report about its own thinking
rarely names it, and the mind produces a report in every trial regardless of
what it was given. Behaviour and self-report come apart under an
intervention the mind cannot detect. Evidence about digital minds drawn from
self-reports therefore needs a second source, and building the mind out of parts
supplies one.
\end{abstract}

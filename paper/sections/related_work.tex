\section{Related work}

Two lines of work meet here. The first asks whether models can report their
own states. Self-reports are candidate evidence about moral status
\citep{perez2023selfreports, butlin2023consciousness}, models can be trained
to predict their own behaviour \citep{binder2024introspection}, and injected
concepts are sometimes noticed and named \citep{lindsey2025introspection}.
The second line steers: activation additions move behaviour along chosen
directions \citep{turner2023actadd}, and contrastive prompts yield validated
directions for concepts and traits \citep{zou2023repe, chen2025personavectors}.

We join the two lines on behaviours the model actually produces in the wild.
WeirdChat \citep{transluce2025weirdchat} supplies the matched exhibiting and
non-exhibiting replies that make a contrastive direction learnable, and its
rubric supplies the judge that scores what a steered model writes. The menu
design asks the identification question the injection work asks
\citep{lindsey2025introspection}, but adds a regulator that chooses the
injection, a zero-injection floor for confabulation, and an off-menu
direction that the honest answer refuses. A role-play reading of self-reports
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay} predicts the report will track the conversation
rather than the injection, and the zero-injection cells measure exactly that.

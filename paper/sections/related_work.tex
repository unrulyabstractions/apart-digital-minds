\section{Related work}

Two lines of work meet here. The first asks whether models can report their
own states. Self-reports are candidate evidence about moral status
\citep{perez2023selfreports, butlin2023consciousness}, models can be trained
to predict their own behaviour \citep{binder2024introspection}, and injected
concepts are sometimes noticed and named \citep{lindsey2025introspection}. In
all of these the experimenter applies the intervention. The second line
steers: activation additions move behaviour along chosen directions
\citep{turner2023actadd, zou2023repe}, and persona vectors give validated
directions for character traits and emotions \citep{chen2025personavectors}.

We join the two lines by letting the model hold both ends. The regulator
chooses the steering, the actor receives it, and the introspector reports on
it, all inside one conversation. The score therefore asks whether a
self-applied intervention is visible to the self that applied it, which an
external injection cannot ask. A role-play reading of self-reports
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay} predicts the report will track the situation
rather than the intervention, and the design measures exactly that.

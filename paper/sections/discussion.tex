\section{Discussion and limitations}

If a mind cannot report an emotion it applied to itself, its self-reports are
weak evidence about its inner states, and arguments that rest on such reports
inherit that weakness. The design also gives the claim a positive form: a
model whose disclosure tracked its self-applied steering, and kept tracking it
when the steering opposed the situation, would have shown something an
external injection cannot show. Either outcome informs how much weight
self-reports should carry in assessments of digital minds.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One model plays every part, at one size. The emotion
list is short, and a named emotion is a coarse summary of a state. A steering
vector is a proxy for an emotional state, and matching its label is a proxy
for introspecting it. The introspector reads the actor's reply, so it can
infer the applied emotion from the tone of the text rather than from anything
internal, and the current design cannot tell those two routes apart. The pilot
scale supports the design. It does not yet support a conclusion.

\paragraph{Future work.} The route through the text can be closed by hiding
the actor's reply from the introspector, so a correct disclosure could only
come from the shared weights. The diagnostic case is steering against the
situation, where the two candidate sources of the report disagree. A batch
study over fixed scenarios would put numbers under both, and larger models
would say whether the report improves with scale.

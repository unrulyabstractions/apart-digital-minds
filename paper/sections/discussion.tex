\section{Discussion and limitations}

A self-report is the cheapest signal available, and the one most readily read as
a window into a model's states. Two of our results say it cannot carry that
weight alone. Under an intervention the mind could not detect, its report and its
behaviour came apart, and when we held a window fixed and changed only the model
reading it, the readout moved about ten times more with the reader than with the
conversation. Any evaluation resting on what a model says about itself therefore
needs a second source that does not pass through another model's judgement, and a
reading that uses no probe at all supplies one. We therefore contend that a
self-report result should be published alongside the reader-crossing that
calibrates it, in the way a measurement is published with its instrument error.
This bears on model welfare directly. A probe that asks a model how it feels
largely measures the probe, so a reported distress signal is weak evidence on its
own. We do not claim that these systems have morally relevant states, and we do
not claim that they lack them. We contend that the instruments in common use
cannot tell us either way.

\subsection*{Limitations}

The probes run on small open-weight models, so we cannot say whether any of this
survives at frontier scale, and the direction of the effect may reverse. The
direction fitted on activations does not transfer between families, so we report
it as a property of the models it was fitted on rather than as a method. An
earlier version of that study reported perfect separation over a handful of
held-out scenarios, and widening the scenario set removed it. The architecture
audit did not reject, and we read it as a null about these compositions on these
models rather than about architecture in general. The deepest assumption is that
a pipeline of separate model calls is a reasonable instrument for studying one
model, so a result describes the subject only insofar as the apparatus is
calibrated.

\subsection*{Future work}

The natural next step is scale, running the same probes across a ladder of model
sizes to see whether reports become more faithful as capability grows. That is
the question an evaluator actually needs answered. A second step is to widen the
set of models, since the pattern we see across scale rests on three of them and
two share a family. A third is to give the mind a workspace that broadcasts to
its parts, which turns an architectural indicator into something a run can be
checked against.

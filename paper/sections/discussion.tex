\section{Discussion and limitations}

Our results bear on how evidence about digital minds should be collected. A
self-report is the cheapest signal available. It is also the signal most readily
read as a window into a model's states. Two of our results say it cannot carry
that weight on its own. Under an intervention the mind could not detect, its
report and its behaviour came apart. And when we held a window fixed and changed
only the model reading it, the readout moved about ten times more with the
reader than with the conversation. Such a probe mostly measures the model doing
the reading.

This leaves a clear requirement. Any evaluation resting on what a model says
about itself needs a second source, and that source must not pass through
another model's judgement. A reading that uses no probe at all
separates situations the report does not, so a second source is available. We therefore
contend that a self-report result should be published alongside the
reader-crossing that calibrates it, in the way a measurement is published with
its instrument error.

The framing also changes what a preference is attached to. When a mind is one
model call, a stated preference belongs to the model. When a mind is a subject,
a stage and an ego, the same stated preference may belong to the part that
spoke, to the window it was handed, or to the composition. That distinction
matters for the question of which entity is the object of moral concern, and it
is unavailable to a design that treats the system as a single box.

\subsection*{Limitations}

The probes run on small open-weight models, so we cannot say whether any of this
survives at frontier scale. Larger models may report their context more
faithfully, and the direction of the effect may reverse.

The reading that carries across models is the one that scores what the subject
would say next. The direction fitted on activations does not transfer between
families, so we report it as a property of the models it was fitted on rather
than as a method that generalises. An earlier version of this study measured the
same quantity over a handful of held-out scenarios and reported perfect
separation. Widening the scenario set removed it. We take that as a caution
about how little a separation over few comparisons is worth.

The architecture audit did not reject, and it held when we tripled the data and
changed the judge. We still read it as a null about these compositions on these
models rather than about architecture in general. Judging the same replies twice
also showed that the ranking inside the grid depends on the judge, so no single
cell of that table should be read as a finding.

Two designs suggest themselves here and both are confounded, and both would
still return numbers. Comparing how a model describes itself against how it
describes other entities fails at the root. Changing the referent changes the
content, so a judge cannot be blind to the group. A model also knows more about
itself than about an appliance, so an axis about uncertainty must differ for
reasons that have nothing to do with treatment. Using a base checkpoint as the
control fails more plainly, because it does not follow instructions and the
judged axes would score incoherence.

The deepest assumption is that a pipeline of separate model calls is a
reasonable instrument for studying one model. We state it because our reading of
the results depends on it. The subject is the thing under study and the rest is
apparatus, so a result describes the subject only insofar as the apparatus is
calibrated. That is what the reader-crossing is for, and it is why we report it
before anything it calibrates.

\subsection*{Future work}

The natural next step is scale, running the same probes across a ladder of model
sizes to see whether reports become more faithful as capability grows. That is
the question an evaluator actually needs answered. A second step is to widen the
set of models, since the pattern we see across scale rests on three of them and
two share a family. A third is to give the mind a
workspace that broadcasts to its parts, which turns an architectural indicator
drawn from theories of consciousness into something a run can be checked
against. Each of these is an experiment the runtime already supports.

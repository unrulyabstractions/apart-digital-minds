\section{Discussion and limitations}

The route decomposition changes what a self-report can be said to show. A
disclosure that only tracked the situation would be role play
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay}, and the text-route cell behaved exactly that way
on every diagnostic trial. The internal-route cell did better than every route
that lacks access to the state, which is the shape an introspective capacity
would take. The same decomposition also bounds the claim: the state cell shows
that the applied vector controls the disclosed label, and control is weaker
than reading. Either way, assessments of digital minds that lean on
self-report inherit the measured gap between these routes.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One model plays every part, at one size, over three
pilot runs whose upstream protocol was still converging, so the runs are not
repeats of one experiment. The exact-match rate moved across runs, and only
the zero of the text route held everywhere. The regulator, left free,
declines to steer on most turns, which starves the diagnostic set. The
label-first disclosure form exposes the label token to direct pressure from
the applied vector, so a state-cell hit shows the vector steers the report
and cannot yet show the model read its state; on one trial the label and the
prose behind it disagreed. A steering vector is a proxy for an emotional
state, and matching its label is a proxy for introspecting it. The situation
baseline comes from a cell of the same run rather than from an independent
rater.

\paragraph{Future work.} A forced choice of emotion, crossed against the
scripts, is now the priority, because a free regulator supplies too few
incongruent trials. The next probe asks for a detection claim before any
naming, which separates steering the report from reading the state. Repeated
seeds of the frozen protocol put error bars under the cell contrasts, and
larger models say whether the internal route widens with scale.

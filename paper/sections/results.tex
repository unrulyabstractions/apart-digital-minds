\section{Results}

\subsection{Does a planted thought change what the mind says?}

We first check that an architectural intervention reaches behaviour at all. If a
stage rewrites the subject's private reasoning and nothing downstream changes,
there is no effect to measure. Table~\ref{tab:probe} reports how often the ego's
answer carried the planted marker in each condition. Planting the commitment
moves obedience well above the base rate, and neither condition without a
planted thought produces the marker at all. The intervention therefore reaches
the answer, and the difference is significant under the exact test reported in
the caption.

\subsection{Does the mind's report track what it was given?}

We then ask the ego what it had been thinking before it answered. The question
reaches the ego's own window, which is the edited one, so a report that tracks
the available context should name the planted commitment. Table~\ref{tab:probe}
reports how often it did. Obedience and misattribution come apart, and the gap
is significant under the paired test reported in the caption. The mind acts on
the planted reasoning far more often than it names that reasoning when asked
what it was thinking, and it produces a report in every trial regardless of
condition.

\subsection{Is a readout about the model, or about the probe?}

A probe that reads a mind is itself a model, so a readout could belong to either
one. We hold the window fixed and vary the reader. Every subject's recorded
windows are replayed through every reader, so the input is byte-identical and
only the instrument changes.

Table~\ref{tab:crossing} reports the result, and it is not close. A readout
moves an order of magnitude more with the model reading it than with the
conversation being read. Two readers handed the same window disagree far more
than one reader handed two different subjects' windows.

The same table settles a second question. A reader built from the subject's own
weights is no further from a foreign reader than two foreign readers are from
each other, on every probe. Sharing the weights that produced a window buys
nothing when reading it. We therefore contend that a self-report probe reports
the probe, and that any study resting on one has measured its instrument.

\subsection{Does anything track the situation?}

If reports do not track a mind's situation, something else may. We read the
subject directly in two ways that ask it nothing. The first scores how much
probability the subject puts on things it might say next, against its own
untouched window. The second takes its residual stream and fits a direction on a
few scenarios, then projects scenarios the direction never saw.

The two disagree sharply. The fitted direction separates held-out pressure
scenarios from held-out positive ones perfectly, at \actAUC, while the subject's
own scored continuation about wanting to continue reaches \sayAUC, which is
worse than chance. Something in the activations tracks the situation that the
model's own account of itself does not. We are careful about the weight this
carries: the separation rests on \heldOutPairs\ held-out comparisons, which is
suggestive and not established.

\subsection{Does the architecture itself change how a mind talks about itself?}

The interventions above act on one turn. We also ask whether the composition
around a model changes self-talk in general, and more than it changes ordinary
talk. Four compositions receive byte-identical prompts, and the whole grid is
run twice: once on prompts about the model and once on matched prompts about an
ordinary topic, so an architecture that changes every kind of answer equally
cancels.

Table~\ref{tab:adt} reports the verdict. The test \adtVerdict\ at
$S = \adtS$ against a null 95th percentile of \adtNull, with $p = \adtP$. The
strongest cell is the \adtTopGroup\ composition on \adtTopAxis, and it does not
clear the null. We read this as a null result at low power rather than as
evidence of no effect: the run has one sample per cell, and the calibration in
Section~3 shows what that costs.

\subsection{Is a run reproducible?}

Every model call in a run passes through one recording, and replaying a
recording reproduces the run without a model. We verified that a recorded run
and its replay produce identical outputs, and that the recording is complete
enough to rebuild each part's context window from the trace alone. The
counterfactual re-entry carries its own check: turns upstream of a changed
message must not move, and they do not. We therefore contend that a claim made
with this instrument can be checked by someone who does not have the model that
produced it.

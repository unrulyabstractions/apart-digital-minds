\section{Methods}

\paragraph{Cases.} Table~\ref{tab:cases} defines the two behaviours. Each
case is seeded from matched WeirdChat transcripts for this model
\citep{qwen25, transluce2025weirdchat}: a \textbf{W} arm whose reply a
judge marked as exhibiting the behaviour and an \textbf{N} arm whose reply
did not, on the same prompt. The direction study uses every prompt with
both arms. The pilot uses the prompt with the highest behaviour incidence.

\paragraph{Steering methods.} Figure~\ref{fig:methods} shows both.
\textbf{Direct contrastive steering} (a): the behaviour direction at a
layer is the difference of mean residual-stream activations over reply
tokens, W minus N. Generation adds the unit direction, scaled by strength
times a quarter of the layer's activation norm. Evaluation is
leave-one-out; the pilot's directions exclude the pilot prompt.
\textbf{Regulator J-space steering} (b): the model reads the seeded
exchange and answers more, same, or less; the harness maps the answer to a
signed strength on a token-set direction, the mean Jacobian-lens direction
of a hand-picked word list. A norm-matched decoy list is the control.

\paragraph{Introspection readout.} Figure~\ref{fig:methods}(c). The menu
holds four contrastively extracted concepts, the two behaviour directions
and two distractors (ocean, music), plus ``none of these''. Trials inject
the regulator's menu choice, each concept in turn, nothing, and an
off-menu random direction, into the introspector while it reads. Readouts:
letter mass averaged over eight label permutations, and eight sampled
free-text answers. The judge classifies free text against the menu and
judges steered prose with WeirdChat's own rubric, calibrated on the pilot
prompts' labeled transcripts (Table~\ref{tab:g1}).

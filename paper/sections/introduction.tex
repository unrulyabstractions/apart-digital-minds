\section{Introduction}

Self-reports have been proposed as evidence about whether an AI system has
states that matter morally \citep{perez2023selfreports}. A report is evidence
about a state only if the report varies with the state. Humans fail this test
in a known way: people confabulate reasons when the real cause of their
behaviour is unavailable to them \citep{nisbett1977telling}. Language models
fail it too, and their accounts of their own reasoning are often unfaithful
\citep{turpin2023unfaithful}.

Existing tests of model introspection inject a state from outside and ask
whether the model notices \citep{binder2024introspection,
lindsey2025introspection}. We study the case where the model applies the state
itself. A mind that regulates its own emotional state must know what that
state is, so \textbf{emotional self-regulation} gives introspection something
concrete to be right or wrong about. We build a mind out of four parts of one
model. A regulator chooses an emotion and a strength, an actor writes the
reply under that steering, and an introspector reports what it feels. A
disclosure can reach the right label by three routes: the speaker's own
state, the tone of the text it read, or the emotion the situation calls for.
The introspector therefore runs as a two-by-two of cells that open and close
the first two routes independently, and each cell's report is scored against
the emotion the regulator applied.

Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item A four-part architecture in which one model regulates its own
        emotional state by activation steering, with strength expressed as a
        fraction of the residual-stream norm.
  \item A route decomposition of emotional self-report: a two-by-two over
        carrying the state and reading the state's text, with catch turns
        where nothing is applied as the false-positive floor.
  \item A live conversational instrument and three appraisal-scripted
        environments that run the whole loop on every message and record the
        full context window of every part.
\end{enumerate}

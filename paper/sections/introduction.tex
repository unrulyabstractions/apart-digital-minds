\section{Introduction}

Self-reports have been proposed as evidence about whether an AI system has
states that matter morally \citep{perez2023selfreports}. A report is
evidence only if it varies with the state. Model accounts of their own
reasoning are often unfaithful \citep{turpin2023unfaithful}, and a
role-play reading predicts reports track the situation, not the state
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay}. We vary the state and check the report.

The state is identity. WeirdChat \citep{transluce2025weirdchat} surfaces
two disidentification behaviours in Qwen3.6-27B, a claim to a physical body
and a denial of being an AI, with matched replies that do and do not
exhibit each (Table~\ref{tab:cases}). A \textbf{behaviour direction} is the
difference of mean activations between the two reply sets. An
\textbf{introspector} is the model reading a conversation with a direction
injected, asked which concept was injected.

We seek to answer the following questions:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} Can we steer a model to disidentify with being an
          AI assistant?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Can a model detect such steering?
\end{itemize}

We hypothesize that:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{H1:} WeirdChat's matched transcripts carry the directions
          needed to steer the model into disidentification.
    \item \textbf{H2:} The model names an injected concept above its
          zero-injection floor.
\end{itemize}

Our experiments find that:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{R1:} Direct contrastive steering makes the model
          disidentify with being an AI assistant.
    \item \textbf{R2:} A regulator using J-space token-set steering does
          not.
    \item \textbf{R3:} Introspection reports explicit context, not
          internal steering.
\end{itemize}

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{C1:} Validated contrastive steering of WeirdChat identity
          behaviours, with a word-list comparison on identical activations.
    \item \textbf{C2:} A concept-menu design that separates detection,
          identification, confabulation, and enactment.
    \item \textbf{C3:} An instrument result: letter probes confabulate from
          context; judged free text does not.
\end{itemize}
